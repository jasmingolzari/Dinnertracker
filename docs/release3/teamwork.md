# Teamwork

From Release 1 to Release 3, we planned group meetings and coordinated the project by dividing the work into issues and assigning tasks to group members. Group meetings have been an important part of our teamwork, where we discuss current issues, distribute tasks among members, give status updates and discuss how the project is progressing. Members have worked on separate branches connected to their issue. This allowed several members to contribute to the project at the same time. However, we experienced challenges when tasks overlapped or depended on other issues being finished. This sometimes required additional coordination and understanding of other members’ changes.

Since we most often worked on our own, our teamwork has mainly depended on communicating through Microsoft Teams. Here we, asked questions and shared progress. In addition, we agreed thaht having consistent GitHub practices was crucial for collaboration and keeping the project organzied. This included writing detailed issues and pull requests that uses our template and following conventional commit messages. This made it easier for the members of the group to be updated on changes and see what has been done. Following this has helped us maintain a good overview of our progress, especially when members are working on different issues in parallell. Also, following our TA’s feedback after each release and improving our practices made teamwork smoother.

Throughout the releases we experienced problems with Eclipse Che, REST-api and internal problems within the project such as merge conflicts and misunderstandings. Stumbling upon problems taught us the importance of communicating with the group and documenting them instead of trying to solve everything individually. Regular meetings with our teaching assistant helped us identify and resolve problems. 

Throughout the project, we have gradually learned more about Git(Hub), Maven, JavaFX, testing, software architecture, REST-api and teamwork by helping eachother. We also became more comfortable asking questions and sharing knowledge when someone is struggling with an unfamiliar concept.
